Activity 15.1.1.

Consider the vectors \(\uvec{v}_1 = \threevec{1}{0}{1}\text{,}\) \(\uvec{v}_2 = \threevec{1}{1}{2}\text{,}\) and \(\uvec{v}_3 = \threevec{1}{-1}{0}\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

Do you remember what \(\spn\) means? Explain why the vector
\begin{equation*} \uvec{x} = 3\uvec{v}_1 + 2\uvec{v}_2 - \uvec{v}_3 \end{equation*}
is in \(\spn \{\uvec{v}_1,\uvec{v}_2,\uvec{v}_3\}\text{.}\)
🔗

Aside: Note.

You can compute \(\uvec{x}\) if you like, but it is not necessary.
🔗
🔗

(b)

Actually, \(\uvec{v}_2\) can be expressed as a linear combination of \(\uvec{v}_1\) and \(\uvec{v}_3\) — do you see how?
🔗
Use this and the expression for \(\uvec{x}\) in Task a to express \(\uvec{x}\) as a linear combination of just \(\uvec{v}_1\) and \(\uvec{v}_3\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(c)

Task b shows that \(\uvec{x}\) is in \(\spn \{\uvec{v}_1,\uvec{v}_3\}\text{.}\) Do you think that similar calculations and the same reasoning can be carried out for every vector in \(\spn \{\uvec{v}_1,\uvec{v}_2,\uvec{v}_3\}\text{?}\)
🔗
What does this say about \(\spn \{\uvec{v}_1,\uvec{v}_2,\uvec{v}_3\}\) versus \(\spn \{\uvec{v}_1,\uvec{v}_3\}\text{?}\)
🔗
