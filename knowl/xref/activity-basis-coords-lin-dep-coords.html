Activity 16.0.2.

Consider the set \(S = \left\{\twovec{1}{0},\twovec{1}{1},\twovec{1}{-1}\right\}\) of vectors in \(\bbR^2\text{.}\) This set spans \(\bbR^2\) but is not linearly independent.
🔗

(a)

Since \(S\) spans \(\bbR^2\text{,}\) it is possible to express vector \(\twovec{3}{-3}\) as a linear combination of the vectors in \(S\text{.}\)
🔗
Demonstrate a way to do this:
\begin{equation*} \twovec{3}{-3} \; = \; \fillinmath{XX}\twovec{1}{0} \; + \; \fillinmath{XX}\twovec{1}{1} \; + \; \fillinmath{XX}\twovec{1}{-1}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

(b)

Here is the redundant part. Demonstrate a different way to express \(\twovec{3}{-3}\) as a linear combination of the vectors in \(S\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \twovec{3}{-3} \; = \; \fillinmath{XX}\twovec{1}{0} \; + \; \fillinmath{XX}\twovec{1}{1} \; + \; \fillinmath{XX}\twovec{1}{-1}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

(c)

How many different ways do you think there are to do this?
